# Modulation & Multiplexing

At the **Physical Layer (Layer 1)**, data is transmitted in the form of signals.

Computers generate **digital data (0s and 1s)**, but transmission media (copper, fiber, wireless) carry signals in electrical, light, or electromagnetic form.

To transmit data properly, we use:

* **Encoding / Conversion Techniques**
* **Modulation Techniques**
* **Multiplexing Techniques**

These help convert and combine signals for efficient transmission.

---

## 1. Digital to Digital Conversion

Digital-to-Digital conversion means:

> Converting digital data (0s and 1s) into digital signals suitable for transmission over a medium.

The signal remains digital, but its representation changes.

---

### Line Coding

Line coding converts binary data into electrical pulses.

### Common Line Coding Techniques

### 1. NRZ (Non-Return to Zero)

* 1 → High voltage
* 0 → Low voltage

Problem:

* No synchronization if long sequence of 0s or 1s.

---

### 2. Manchester Encoding

* 0 → High to Low transition
* 1 → Low to High transition

Advantages:

* Self-synchronizing
* Clock recovery possible

Used in Ethernet (older versions).

---

### 3. Differential Manchester

* Transition at beginning of bit = 0
* No transition at beginning = 1

More reliable against polarity changes.

---

### 1.3 Block Coding

Adds extra bits to improve synchronization and error detection.

Example:

4B/5B coding

Every 4 bits replaced by 5 bits.

Used in Fast Ethernet.

---

### 1.4 Scrambling

Used to avoid long sequences of 0s or 1s.

Example:

* B8ZS
* HDB3

---

### Key Purpose of Digital-to-Digital Conversion

* Synchronization
* Error detection
* DC balance
* Efficient bandwidth usage

---

## 2. Digital to Analog Conversion

Digital data is converted into analog signals so that it can be transmitted over:

* Telephone lines
* Wireless channels
* Radio systems

This process is called **Modulation**.

---

### Types of Digital-to-Analog Modulation

---

### 1. ASK (Amplitude Shift Keying)

Binary data changes amplitude of carrier wave.

* 1 → High amplitude
* 0 → Low amplitude

Simple but noise-prone.

---

### 2. FSK (Frequency Shift Keying)

Binary data changes frequency.

* 1 → High frequency
* 0 → Low frequency

More reliable than ASK.

Used in early modems.

---

### 3. PSK (Phase Shift Keying)

Binary data changes phase of carrier.

* 0° → 0
* 180° → 1

More noise resistant.

---

### 4. QAM (Quadrature Amplitude Modulation)

Combination of:

* Amplitude variation
* Phase variation

Allows multiple bits per symbol.

Example:

16-QAM → 4 bits per symbol
64-QAM → 6 bits per symbol

Used in:

* Wi-Fi
* Cable TV
* LTE

---

## 3. Analog to Digital Conversion

Converting analog signals (voice, music) into digital form.

Required in:

* Mobile communication
* VoIP
* Digital recording

---

### PCM (Pulse Code Modulation)

Steps:

1. Sampling
2. Quantization
3. Encoding

---

### Step 1: Sampling

Signal measured at regular intervals.

According to Nyquist Theorem:

Sampling rate ≥ 2 × maximum signal frequency

Example:

Voice bandwidth ≈ 4 kHz
Minimum sampling rate = 8 kHz

---

### Step 2: Quantization

Amplitude values approximated into discrete levels.

More levels → Better quality → More bits required.

---

### Step 3: Encoding

Each quantized value converted into binary code.

Standard Telephone PCM:

* 8000 samples/sec
* 8 bits per sample

Bit rate:

8000 × 8 = 64 kbps

This is standard digital voice channel.

---

## 3.3 Delta Modulation

Instead of encoding absolute values, it encodes change in amplitude.

Simpler but less accurate.

---

## 4. Analog to Analog Conversion

Used when transmitting analog signals over bandpass channels.

Example:

Radio broadcasting.

---

### Types of Analog Modulation

---

### 1. AM (Amplitude Modulation)

Amplitude of carrier wave changes according to signal.

Used in AM radio.

---

### 2. FM (Frequency Modulation)

Frequency of carrier wave changes.

Better noise resistance.

Used in FM radio.

---

### 3. PM (Phase Modulation)

Phase of carrier changes according to signal.

---

## 5. Multiplexing

Multiplexing allows:

> Multiple signals to share a single communication channel.

Instead of one user per channel, multiple users can transmit simultaneously.

---

## Types of Multiplexing

### 1. FDM (Frequency Division Multiplexing)

Each signal assigned different frequency band.

Used in:

* Radio broadcasting
* Cable TV

---

### 2. TDM (Time Division Multiplexing)

Each signal assigned specific time slot.

Used in:

* Telephone networks
* Digital communication

Two types:

* Synchronous TDM
* Statistical TDM

---

### 3. WDM (Wavelength Division Multiplexing)

Used in fiber optic communication.

Different wavelengths (colors) carry different signals.

Enables very high capacity transmission.

---

### 4. CDM (Code Division Multiplexing)

Each user assigned unique code.

Used in:

* 3G mobile networks

---

## 6. Modulation vs Multiplexing

| Feature  | Modulation          | Multiplexing             |
| -------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Purpose  | Convert signal form | Combine multiple signals |
| Used For | Signal transmission | Channel sharing          |
| Example  | ASK, FM             | FDM, TDM                 |
| Layer    | Physical            | Physical                 |

---

## Key Takeaways

* Digital-to-Digital involves line coding and block coding.

* Digital-to-Analog uses ASK, FSK, PSK, QAM.

* Analog-to-Digital uses PCM and sampling.

* Analog-to-Analog uses AM, FM, PM.

* Multiplexing allows multiple users to share bandwidth.

* QAM and WDM are heavily used in modern high-speed communication systems.
