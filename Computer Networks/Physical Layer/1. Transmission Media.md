# Physical Layer Transmission Media

**Transmission Media** refers to the physical path through which data travels from sender to receiver at the **Physical Layer (Layer 1)** of the OSI model.

It carries **raw bits (0s and 1s)** in the form of electrical signals, light pulses, or radio waves.

Transmission media is broadly classified into:

1. **Guided Media (Wired)**
2. **Unguided Media (Wireless)**

---

## 1. Guided Media (Wired Transmission)

Guided media uses a **physical cable or conductor** to transmit signals.

### 1.1 Twisted Pair Cable


**Definition:**
Two insulated copper wires twisted together to reduce electromagnetic interference.

### Types:

1. **UTP (Unshielded Twisted Pair)**
2. **STP (Shielded Twisted Pair)**

### Categories:

Cat5, Cat5e, Cat6, Cat6a, Cat7, Cat8

### Characteristics:

* Cheap and easy to install
* Used in LANs
* Susceptible to noise (UTP)
* Distance limit ≈ 100 meters
* Supports speeds up to 10 Gbps (Cat6/6a)

### Applications:

* Ethernet LANs
* Telephone lines

---

### 1.2 Coaxial Cable

**Definition:**
A copper conductor surrounded by insulation, metallic shield, and outer cover.

### Characteristics:

* Better shielding than twisted pair
* Higher bandwidth
* Less noise interference
* More expensive than UTP
* Distance ≈ 500 meters (varies)

### Applications:

* Cable TV
* Broadband Internet
* CCTV systems

---

### 1.3 Optical Fiber Cable

**Definition:**
Transmits data using **light signals** through glass or plastic fibers.

### Types:

1. **Single Mode Fiber (SMF)**
2. **Multi Mode Fiber (MMF)**

### Characteristics:

* Very high bandwidth
* Extremely low signal loss
* Immune to electromagnetic interference
* Very secure
* Long-distance transmission (100+ km)

### Speed:

* 10 Gbps, 40 Gbps, 100 Gbps and beyond

### Applications:

* Backbone networks
* Undersea cables
* Data centers

---

## 2. Unguided Media (Wireless Transmission)

Unguided media transmits signals through **air or vacuum** using electromagnetic waves.

---

### 2.1 Radio Waves

**Frequency Range:** 3 kHz – 1 GHz

### Characteristics:

* Omnidirectional
* Can penetrate walls
* Long-distance communication
* Susceptible to interference

### Applications:

* AM/FM radio
* Wi-Fi
* Mobile communication

---

### 2.2 Microwaves

**Frequency Range:** 1 GHz – 300 GHz

### Characteristics:

* Directional (line-of-sight required)
* High bandwidth
* Used for long-distance communication

### Types:

1. Terrestrial microwave
2. Satellite microwave

### Applications:

* Cellular networks
* Satellite TV
* Point-to-point communication

---

### 2.3 Infrared

**Frequency Range:** 300 GHz – 400 THz

### Characteristics:

* Short-range communication
* Cannot penetrate walls
* Secure within confined space

### Applications:

* TV remotes
* Short-range device communication

---

## 3. Comparison Table

| Media Type    | Bandwidth | Cost   | Distance  | Noise Immunity | Security  |
| ------------- | --------- | ------ | --------- | -------------- | --------- |
| Twisted Pair  | Medium    | Low    | Low       | Low-Medium     | Low       |
| Coaxial Cable | High      | Medium | Medium    | Medium-High    | Medium    |
| Optical Fiber | Very High | High   | Very High | Very High      | Very High |
| Radio Waves   | Medium    | Low    | High      | Low            | Low       |
| Microwaves    | High      | Medium | High      | Medium         | Medium    |
| Infrared      | Low       | Low    | Very Low  | High (indoor)  | High      |

---

## 4. Key Physical Layer Parameters

When choosing transmission media, consider:

* **Bandwidth**

* **Attenuation (Signal loss)**

* **Noise resistance**

* **Cost**

* **Installation difficulty**

* **Distance coverage**

* **Security**

---

## 5. Important Interview Concepts

### 1. Attenuation

Loss of signal strength over distance.

### 2. Noise

Unwanted disturbance affecting signal quality.

### 3. Bandwidth

Maximum data carrying capacity of a medium.

### 4. Throughput

Actual data transmitted per second.

### 5. EMI (Electromagnetic Interference)

Interference caused by nearby electrical signals.

---

## 6. Real-World Examples

* LAN → Twisted Pair (Cat6)
* Internet Backbone → Optical Fiber
* Mobile Networks → Microwaves + Fiber
* Satellite TV → Satellite Microwave

---

## Key Takeaways

* Transmission media operates at the **Physical Layer (Layer 1)**

* Classified into **Guided (Wired)** and **Unguided (Wireless)**

* Fiber offers highest bandwidth and security

* Twisted pair is most common in LANs

* Wireless media provides mobility but is more prone to interference

* Media choice depends on cost, distance, and bandwidth requirements
