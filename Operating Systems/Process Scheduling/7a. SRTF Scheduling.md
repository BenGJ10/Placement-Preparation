# Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF)

**Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF)** is the **preemptive version of Shortest Job First (SJF)**.  

In this algorithm, the CPU is always allocated to the process with the **smallest remaining execution time**.

SRTF is designed to provide **optimal average waiting time**, even in systems where processes arrive at different times.

---

## 1. What is SRTF Scheduling?

### Definition
In **SRTF scheduling**, the CPU is assigned to the process that has the **least remaining CPU burst time** among all processes currently in the ready queue.

> If a new process arrives with a shorter remaining time than the currently running process, the CPU is **preempted** immediately.

---

## 2. Key Characteristics of SRTF

- **Preemptive** scheduling algorithm
- Extension of **SJF**
- Minimizes **average waiting time**
- Frequent **context switching**
- High risk of **starvation**

---

## 3. How SRTF Works

1. Scheduler selects process with **shortest remaining burst time**

2. If a new process arrives with shorter remaining time:
   - Current process is **preempted**
   - New process is scheduled

3. Process runs until:
   - completion, or
   - another shorter job arrives

---

## 4. SRTF Example

### Given Processes

| Process | Arrival Time | Burst Time |
|--------|--------------|------------|
| P1 | 0 | 8 |
| P2 | 1 | 4 |
| P3 | 2 | 2 |
| P4 | 3 | 1 |

---

### Step-by-Step Execution

#### Time 0
- Only P1 available → execute P1 (remaining = 7)

#### Time 1
- P2 arrives (4 < 7) → preempt P1  
- Execute P2 (remaining = 3)

#### Time 2
- P3 arrives (2 < 3) → preempt P2  
- Execute P3 (remaining = 1)

#### Time 3
- P4 arrives (1 < 1?) → tie-breaker → choose new job  
- Execute P4 → completes

#### Time 4
- Remaining:
  - P3 (1)
  - P2 (3)
  - P1 (7)
- Execute P3 → completes

#### Time 5
- Remaining:
  - P2 (3)
  - P1 (7)
- Execute P2 → completes

#### Time 8
- Execute P1 → completes

---

### Gantt Chart

```

| P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | P3 | P2 | P1 |
0    1    2    3    4    5    8    15

```

---

## 5. Completion Time (CT)

| Process | CT |
|--------|----|
| P4 | 4 |
| P3 | 5 |
| P2 | 8 |
| P1 | 15 |

---

## 6. Turnaround Time (TAT)

```

TAT = CT − Arrival Time

```

| Process | CT | TAT |
|--------|----|-----|
| P1 | 15 | 15 − 0 = 15 |
| P2 | 8 | 8 − 1 = 7 |
| P3 | 5 | 5 − 2 = 3 |
| P4 | 4 | 4 − 3 = 1 |

---

## 7. Waiting Time (WT)

```

WT = TAT − Burst Time

```

| Process | AT | BT | CT | TAT | WT |
|--------|----|----|----|-----|----|
| P1 | 0 | 8 | 15 | 15 | 15 − 8 = 7 |
| P2 | 1 | 4 | 8 | 7 | 7 − 4 = 3 |
| P3 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 3 − 2 = 1 |
| P4 | 3 | 1 | 4 | 1 | 1 − 1 = 0 |

---

### Average Waiting Time

```
(7 + 3 + 1 + 0) / 4 = 2.75
```

This is **lower than FCFS and non-preemptive SJF**.

---

## 8. Starvation in SRTF

### Problem
Long processes may **never execute** if shorter jobs keep arriving.

### Example
- Continuous short jobs → long job stays preempted indefinitely

### Solution
- **Aging** (increase priority over time)
- Hybrid scheduling approaches

---

## 9. Burst Time Prediction

Like SJF, SRTF requires **burst time estimation**.

Uses **exponential averaging**:


---

## 10. Advantages vs Disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| Minimum average waiting time | High context switching overhead |
| Responsive to short jobs | Starvation risk for long jobs |
| Better turnaround time | Complex implementation |
| Preemptive | Requires accurate burst time prediction |

---

## Key Takeaways

- SRTF is the **preemptive form of SJF**

- Always runs the shortest remaining job

- Produces **minimum average waiting time**

- Suffers from **starvation and overhead**

- Suitable mainly for theoretical and batch analysis
