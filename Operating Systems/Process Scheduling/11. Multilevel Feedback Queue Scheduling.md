# Multilevel Feedback Queue Scheduling (MLFQ)

**Multilevel Feedback Queue Scheduling (MLFQ)** is an advanced CPU scheduling algorithm designed to **overcome the limitations of Multilevel Queue Scheduling**.  

It allows processes to **move between queues dynamically** based on their **CPU usage and behavior**.

MLFQ is widely used in **modern operating systems** because it provides a good balance between **responsiveness, fairness, and throughput**.

---

## 1. What is Multilevel Feedback Queue Scheduling?

### Definition
In **Multilevel Feedback Queue Scheduling**, the ready queue is divided into **multiple priority queues**, and:

- processes can **move between queues**
- priority changes dynamically
- scheduling decisions are based on **process behavior**

> The term *feedback* means the scheduler **learns from how processes behave**.

---

## 2. Why MLFQ is Needed

Problems with Multilevel Queue Scheduling:
- Fixed queue assignment
- Starvation of low-priority processes
- No adaptation to process behavior

MLFQ solves this by:
- promoting I/O-bound or interactive processes
- demoting CPU-bound processes
- dynamically adjusting priorities

---

## 3. Basic Structure of MLFQ

- Multiple ready queues (Q0, Q1, Q2, ...)
- Q0 has **highest priority**
- Priority decreases as queue level increases
- Each queue uses a **different scheduling policy**

---

## 4. General Rules of MLFQ

Typical MLFQ rules:

1. **Higher-priority queue always runs first**

2. New processes start in the **highest-priority queue**

3. If a process uses its entire time quantum:
   - it is **demoted** to a lower-priority queue

4. If a process yields CPU early (I/O-bound):
   - it stays or is **promoted**

5. Lower queues have **larger time quanta**

---

## 5. Example Queue Configuration

| Queue | Priority | Scheduling Algorithm | Time Quantum |
|-----|----------|----------------------|--------------|
| Q0 | Highest | Round Robin | 4 ms |
| Q1 | Medium | Round Robin | 8 ms |
| Q2 | Lowest | FCFS | ∞ |

---

## 6. How MLFQ Works (Example Scenario)

### Process Behavior

- Interactive process → short CPU bursts → stays in higher queue
- CPU-bound process → long CPU bursts → moves to lower queue

### Flow
1. Process enters Q0
2. Uses full quantum → demoted to Q1
3. Uses full quantum → demoted to Q2
4. Runs FCFS at lowest priority

---

## 7. Starvation and Its Prevention

### Problem
Low-priority processes may starve if higher queues are busy.

### Solution: Priority Boosting
- Periodically move **all processes to highest queue**
- Ensures fairness

---

## 8. Advantages vs Disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|----------------|
| Adapts to process behavior | Complex to implement |
| Reduces starvation | Requires careful tuning of parameters |
| Balances responsiveness and throughput | Overhead from context switching |
| Suitable for diverse workloads | Difficult to predict performance |

___

## 9. MLQ vs MLFQ

| Feature | Multilevel Queue | MLFQ |
|------|-----------------|------|
| Queue movement | No | Yes |
| Priority | Fixed | Dynamic |
| Starvation | High | Reduced |
| Adaptiveness | No | Yes |

---

## Key Takeaways

- MLFQ adapts to **process behavior**

- Balances responsiveness and throughput

- Reduces starvation through feedback

- Complex but powerful scheduling algorithm

- Foundation of modern OS schedulers
