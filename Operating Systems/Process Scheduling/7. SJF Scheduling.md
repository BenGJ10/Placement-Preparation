# Shortest Job First (SJF)

**Shortest Job First (SJF)** is a CPU scheduling algorithm in which the process with the **smallest CPU burst time** is selected for execution next.  

It is known for producing the **minimum average waiting time**, making it theoretically optimal among scheduling algorithms.

---

## 1. What is SJF Scheduling?

### Definition

In **SJF scheduling**, the CPU is allocated to the process that has the **shortest execution (burst) time** among all processes in the ready queue.

> Scheduling decision is based on **CPU burst time**, not arrival time alone.

---

## 2. Types of SJF Scheduling

SJF can be implemented in two ways:

### 2.1 Non-Preemptive SJF

- Once a process starts executing, it **runs till completion**
- CPU is allocated to the shortest job **available at scheduling time**

### 2.2 Preemptive SJF (SRTF)

- CPU can be taken away if a **shorter job arrives**
- Also called **Shortest Remaining Time First (SRTF)**

*(This section focuses mainly on SJF; SRTF is usually studied separately.)*

---

## 3. Key Characteristics of SJF

- Can be **preemptive or non-preemptive**
- Minimizes **average waiting time**
- Requires knowledge or prediction of CPU burst time
- Risk of **starvation** for long processes
- Not practical without burst-time estimation

---

## 4. How SJF Works (Non-Preemptive)

1. All processes enter the ready queue

2. Scheduler selects the process with **minimum burst time**

3. Selected process executes till completion

4. Scheduler repeats the process

---

## 5. SJF Example

### Given Processes

| Process | Arrival Time | Burst Time |
|--------|--------------|------------|
| P1 | 0 | 8 |
| P2 | 1 | 4 |
| P3 | 2 | 2 |
| P4 | 3 | 1 |

---

### Step 1: Ready Queue Ordering (at time 0)

Only P1 is available → runs first

After P1 completes, all others are available → choose shortest burst

---

### Step 2: Execution Order

```
P1 → P4 → P3 → P2
```

---

### Step 3: Gantt Chart

```
| P1 | P4 | P3 | P2 |
0    8    9    11   15
```

---

### Step 4: Completion Time (CT)

| Process | AT | CT |
|--------|----|----|
| P1 | 0 | 8 |
| P2 | 1 | 15 |
| P3 | 2 | 11 |
| P4 | 3 | 9 |

---

### Step 5: Turnaround Time (TAT)

```
TAT = CT − Arrival Time
```

| Process | AT | CT | TAT |
|--------|----|----|-----|
| P1 | 0 | 8 | 8 − 0 = 8 |
| P2 | 1 | 15 | 15 − 1 = 14 |
| P3 | 2 | 11 | 11 − 2 = 9 |
| P4 | 3 | 9 | 9 − 3 = 6 |

---

### Step 6: Waiting Time (WT)

```
WT = TAT − Burst Time
```

| Process | WT |
|--------|----|
| P1 | 8 − 8 = 0 |
| P4 | 6 − 1 = 5 |
| P3 | 9 − 2 = 7 |
| P2 | 14 − 4 = 10 |

---

### Step 7: Average Times

```
Average Waiting Time = (0 + 5 + 7 + 10) / 4 = 5.5
Average Turnaround Time = (8 + 6 + 9 + 14) / 4 = 9.25
```

---

## 6. Why SJF is Optimal

### Theoretical Property
SJF produces the **minimum average waiting time** for a given set of processes.

### Reason
Short processes are executed early, reducing the waiting time of many processes behind them.

---

## 7. Major Problem with SJF – Starvation

### What is Starvation?
Long CPU-bound processes may **never get CPU** if shorter jobs keep arriving.

### Example
If many short jobs arrive continuously:
- Long job stays in ready queue indefinitely

---

## 8. Burst Time Prediction Problem

### Why It’s Hard
- Actual CPU burst time is **not known in advance**
- OS must **predict** burst time

### Solution
**Exponential Averaging** is used:

```
τₙ₊₁ = α × tₙ + (1 − α) × τₙ
```

Where:
- τₙ₊₁ = next predicted burst
- tₙ = actual last burst
- α = smoothing factor (0 ≤ α ≤ 1)

---

## 9. Advantages vs Disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|------------|---------------|
| Minimum average waiting time | Starvation of long processes |
| Simple to understand | Requires burst time prediction |
| Efficient for batch systems | Not suitable for interactive systems |
| Reduces context switching (non-preemptive) | Complexity in preemptive version |


---

## Key Takeaways

- SJF schedules processes based on **shortest CPU burst**

- It is **theoretically optimal** for waiting time

- Suffers from **starvation**

- Requires **burst-time prediction**

- More suitable for **batch systems** than interactive systems
