# Round Robin (RR)

**Round Robin (RR)** is a **preemptive CPU scheduling algorithm** designed specifically for **time-sharing systems**.  

It ensures **fair CPU allocation** by giving each process a fixed time slice (called **time quantum**) in a cyclic order.

---

## 1. What is Round Robin Scheduling?

### Definition
In **Round Robin scheduling**, each process in the ready queue is assigned the CPU for a **fixed time quantum**.

> If the process does not finish within the time quantum, it is **preempted** and moved to the **end of the ready queue**.

---

## 2. Key Characteristics of Round Robin

- **Preemptive** scheduling
- Based on **time quantum**
- Ensures **fairness**
- Prevents starvation
- Used in **interactive systems**

---

## 3. How Round Robin Works

1. Processes enter the **ready queue** in arrival order

2. Scheduler assigns CPU to the first process for one **time quantum**

3. If process finishes → removed

4. If not finished → preempted and placed at the end of queue

5. Cycle repeats until all processes complete

---

## 4. Time Quantum (Time Slice)

### Definition
**Time quantum** is the **maximum CPU time** a process can use in one turn.

### Choosing Time Quantum

| Quantum Size | Effect |
|-------------|-------|
| Too small | Too many context switches |
| Too large | Behaves like FCFS |
| Ideal | Balanced response & overhead |

Typical value: **10–100 ms**

## 6. Completion Time (CT)

| Process | CT |
|--------|----|
| P3 | 6 |
| P2 | 10 |
| P1 | 11 |

---

## 7. Turnaround Time (TAT)

```
TAT = CT − Arrival Time
```

| Process | AT | CT | TAT |
|--------|----|----|-----|
| P1 | 0 | 11 | 11 − 0 = 11 |
| P2 | 1 | 10 | 10 − 1 = 9 |
| P3 | 2 | 6 | 6 − 2 = 4 |


---

## 8. Waiting Time (WT)

```
WT = TAT − Burst Time
```

| Process | BT | TAT | WT |
|--------|----|-----|----|
| P1 | 5 | 11 | 11 − 5 = 6 |
| P2 | 4 | 9 | 9 − 4 = 5 |
| P3 | 2 | 4 | 4 − 2 = 2 |

---

### Average Waiting Time

```
(6 + 5 + 2) / 3 = 4.33
```

---

## 9. Advantages of Round Robin

| Advantage | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| Fairness | Every process gets CPU |
| No starvation | Guaranteed execution |
| Good response time | Suitable for interactive users |
| Simple | Easy to implement |

---

## 10. Disadvantages of Round Robin

| Disadvantage | Explanation |
|-------------|-------------|
| Context switching overhead | Too small quantum |
| Average waiting time | Higher than SJF |
| Quantum tuning | Difficult |

---

## 11. Where Round Robin is Used

- Time-sharing systems
- Multi-user environments
- Interactive OS (Linux, Unix variants)
- Server systems with concurrent users

---

## Key Takeaways

- Round Robin ensures **fair CPU sharing**

- Time quantum selection is critical

- Prevents starvation

- Ideal for **interactive systems**

- Widely used in modern OS schedulers
